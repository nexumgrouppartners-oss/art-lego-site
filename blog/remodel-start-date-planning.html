Picking a Remodel Start Date in Palm Beach County

By Art Lego LLC · South Florida · November 13, 2026

Pick a start date after permits and association approvals are sorted, not before. Set the date first and every delay lands on your family. Set it last and the calendar rests on what the project needs. Here is how to plan a remodeling project that way in Palm Beach County.

What has to happen before a start date is set?

Four things should be settled first: the scope, the materials, the approvals and the access plan. Until those are clear, any date is a guess.

Scope means you know which rooms are included and what will be opened up. Materials means you have chosen cabinets, tile, fixtures or windows, and you know how long they take to arrive. Approvals means you know which ones your project needs and who must sign off. Access means you know where the crew will park, where supplies will sit and which parts of the house stay usable.

At Art Lego LLC, we start with a walk-through of the space and a free estimate. That visit is where the scope gets written down. It is also where we talk about what could change the calendar.

Do permits or association approvals come first?

Check your association first if you have one, then handle permits. Many associations want to approve exterior changes before anything else moves. A permit application often goes in with those approved plans attached.

Art Lego counts Permits and association approvals as factors in a remodel estimate when the project requires them. That matters for your calendar. These steps take time, and the time is outside the crew's control. A start date that ignores them is a hope, not a plan.

Not every project needs both. An interior bathroom update in Wellington may never touch an association. A new exterior door or a change to the front of the house probably will. Ask early so you know which case you are in.

Here is a simple order that works for most homeowners:

  1. Decide the scope and make a short list of every change you want.
  2. Read your association rules, or call the office, if the change is visible from outside.
  3. Ask your contractor which permits the work needs.
  4. Submit the paperwork and keep copies of everything.
  5. Order long lead items once the plans are settled.
  6. Set the start date after the approvals come back.

How does the season affect the schedule?

South Florida has a wet season and a hurricane season, and both can move a calendar. The wet season usually runs from late spring into fall. Hurricane season runs from June through November. Exterior work feels these months the most.

Rain stops exterior painting, roofing details, stucco patching and anything that needs dry surfaces. A crew may be ready and the weather still says no. Plan extra room around any outdoor part of your project, and do not stack an exterior job right before a hard deadline like a party or a move.

Interior work is steadier. Kitchen and bathroom remodels mostly happen under a roof. Still, deliveries, dust control and open walls are easier to manage when the house can be opened up on a dry day.

Late fall and winter are often calmer for outdoor work in Palm Beach Gardens and Boynton Beach. Many homeowners also book then, so ask about the schedule early.

How do material lead times change the date?

Special order items set the pace for a lot of remodels. Cabinets, custom countertops, certain tile and some windows can take weeks to arrive. Stock items are faster, but they are not always the ones people choose.

The best habit is to pick your major materials before you pick a date. Then ask the supplier for a delivery window. If something is on a long wait, you can choose a similar item or move the start. Either choice is better than a half-finished kitchen waiting on a box.

It also helps to check materials when they arrive, not on the day they are needed. A wrong size or a damaged piece is much easier to fix with time to spare.

What should you ask for in writing?

Ask for the scope, the materials list, who handles permits and approvals, and the plan for the schedule. Written details keep everyone working from the same page. They also make it easy to see what changed if the plan shifts later.

Here are questions worth asking before you sign off on any date:

  • Which permits does this project need, if any?
  • Does my association need to approve the plans?
  • Who submits the paperwork, and who follows up?
  • Which materials have long waits?
  • What happens to the schedule if it rains for several days?
  • How will changes to the scope be written up?

Keep your own folder of these answers. A simple folder with emails, plans and photos saves time when a question comes up halfway through the job.

What if the date slips?

Stay calm and ask what caused it. Most slips come from one of four sources: an approval that took longer than expected, a late delivery, weather or a change in scope. Each one has a different fix.

If an approval is slow, ask for a status check and keep your records handy. If a material is late, ask for a firm delivery window and see whether a similar item is available. If the weather is the cause, shift the outdoor tasks and move indoor tasks up where that is possible.

A change in scope is the one you control. Adding a room, moving a wall or swapping a material mid-project can push the schedule. If you want a change, say so early and ask how it affects the calendar before you decide.

A good contractor tells you about a slip as soon as it happens. Ask how updates will reach you, whether by call or text, so you are never left guessing. Also plan a small cushion for your own life. If you are living in the home during the work, a few extra days is easier to handle when you did not promise anyone a hard date.

Frequently Asked Questions

How early should I start planning a remodel?

Start as soon as you know which rooms you want to change. Early planning gives you time to choose materials, check association rules and learn which approvals your project needs before you commit to a date.

Can I remodel during Florida's rainy season?

Yes, especially interior projects. Exterior work depends more on dry days, so leave extra room in the schedule. Ask your contractor how they plan around weather before you pick a start date.

Does every remodel need an association approval?

No. Many interior projects never involve an association. Exterior changes often do. Read your community rules or call the association office, and ask your contractor which approvals your project needs.

Who handles the permit paperwork?

It depends on the contractor and the project. Ask who submits it and who follows up before you sign. Get the answer in writing so there is no confusion later.
