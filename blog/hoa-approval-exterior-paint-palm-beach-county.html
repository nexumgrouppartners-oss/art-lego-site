HOA Approval for Exterior Paint Colors in Palm Beach County

By Art Lego LLC · South Florida · November 3, 2026

An HOA can have a say in the exterior paint color of your home. Before you buy paint, check your community rules and get approval from the board. A little preparation helps you avoid delays and extra work. Here is how to plan for HOA approval when you paint your home in Palm Beach County.

Start by reading your HOA rules

Every association is different. Some have a short list of approved colors. Others ask for a full color review. Start by finding the governing documents. Look for sections on exterior appearance, architectural review and painting. These sections explain what needs approval and who reviews it.

Write down the steps as you find them. Note the form you need, any fee that applies and the name of the review contact. This gives you a clear plan before you spend time on samples.

Gather color samples that show well

Paint looks different in Florida sun than it does on a small chip from the store. Bright light, strong shadows and salt air can change how a color reads. Ask your painter for large sample boards, or paint a test patch on the wall. Look at each sample in the morning, at midday and in the evening.

Plan to submit your body color, trim color and door color. Forms often ask for each one. Include the brand and the color name or number. If you are using a color family, note the exact shade on the form so the board can see what you mean.

Prepare a simple submission packet

A clear packet makes the board's job easier. Include these items:

  • Photos of the front and side views of your home
  • Your proposed body, trim, door and shutter colors
  • Paint brand and color names or numbers
  • A sample board or photo that shows the colors together
  • A short note on which parts of the home will be painted

Add a short cover note at the top of the packet. Include your name, your address, the date and a one-line summary of the request. A clear cover note helps the reviewer find your information fast, especially when several forms arrive in the same week.

Keep copies of everything you send. If the board asks questions later, you will have the same information ready to share.

Submit early and allow time for review

Boards do not always meet every week. Some review requests only at set meetings. Others use a committee that reviews forms on a schedule. Ask the association how long reviews usually take. Then submit your request well before you want to start painting.

Timing matters in South Florida. Hot, humid months can make painting schedules harder to hold. Build a few extra weeks into your plan, so a review delay does not push your project into a poor weather stretch.

Ask the association if it keeps a list or chart of approved colors. Choosing from that list can make the review simpler. If your favorite color is not on the list, ask whether a close match is acceptable. Many boards are willing to talk through options before a form is filed, so a quick call can save time.

What boards often look at

Board members usually look at how the new colors fit the neighborhood. They may ask whether the colors suit the style of the home. They may also ask how the colors work with nearby homes and the roof. Be ready to explain your choice in a sentence or two.

If the board asks for changes, stay calm. Ask which specific part they want changed. Often a small shift in shade or trim color solves the issue without starting over.

Plan the painting work around approval

Do not schedule the crew until you have written approval. Starting early can lead to a notice to correct the work, and that can cost time and money. Wait for the approval letter or email, and save it with your project file.

Once you have approval, confirm the final colors with your painter. Check that the paint on the job matches the approved sample. A quick review at the start of the job helps prevent surprises later.

Prep work also matters. Before the first coat, the surface should be cleaned, loose paint scraped away and bare spots primed. Ask your painter how prep fits into the schedule, and confirm that the prep plan matches what you described to the board. Clear communication keeps the job on track from the first day to the final coat.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need HOA approval before I paint my house in Palm Beach County?

In many communities, yes. Check your HOA governing documents first. They explain which exterior changes need approval and who reviews them.

What should I include in an HOA paint color request?

Include photos of the home, the body, trim, door and shutter colors, the brand and color names or numbers, and a sample board or photo that shows the colors together.

How early should I submit my exterior paint request?

Submit early. Ask the association how long reviews take, and leave extra weeks in your plan in case the board asks for changes.
