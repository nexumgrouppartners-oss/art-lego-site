What to Expect During a Whole-Home Repaint in South Florida

By Art Lego LLC · South Florida · November 4, 2026

A whole-home repaint is a big project, but it does not have to feel chaotic. Most jobs follow the same steps: prep, masking, painting, and touch-ups. Knowing what comes next helps you plan your week and your home life.

Start With a Plan and a Walkthrough

Before any paint goes on the wall, the team should walk through your home with you. This is the time to talk about colors, sheens, and which rooms matter most to you. Write down any special areas, like a kids room, a home office, or a front entry that gets a lot of traffic.

Ask questions here. A good painter will tell you how the work will flow from room to room. They will also explain what they plan to protect and what you should move out of the way. It is also smart to ask where the crew will store their supplies, how they will keep work areas tidy at the end of each day, and where they will park while on your property.

Moving Furniture and Clearing Space

Plan to clear each room before work starts there. Take down wall art, curtains, and rods. Pull small items off shelves and counters. Move large furniture to the center of the room and cover it with plastic or drop cloths.

If you have a lot of furniture, you can ask your painter for help. Many homeowners find it easier to pack one room at a time. This keeps the rest of the house usable while the work moves along. Label boxes with the room name so you can find things quickly when it is time to move everything back.

Protecting Floors, Fixtures, and Trim

Good masking is the difference between a clean finish and a messy one. Painters tape off trim, windows, door frames, outlets, and switch plates. They also cover floors and any fixtures they cannot remove. In a home with tile, wood, or carpet, this step matters a lot.

You may see more tape and plastic than you expected. That is normal. It is a sign the crew is taking care of your home.

Prep Work Comes Before Paint

Prep is the quiet part of the job. Walls get patched, sanded, and cleaned. Nail holes and cracks are filled. Surfaces are primed when needed, especially if you are changing from a dark color to a light one or covering stains.

Prep can look slow from the outside. Still, it makes the final coat look smooth and even. Skipping prep is one of the most common reasons a fresh paint job looks flawed after a few months.

How the Painting Itself Usually Goes

Most rooms need more than one coat. The first coat often looks patchy, and that is normal. The second coat brings the color to its full depth and covers the surface evenly. Ceilings usually get painted first, then walls, then trim and doors. This order keeps drips off freshly painted walls.

Each room needs time to dry between coats. Your painter will plan this so the work keeps moving. Good light helps you see uneven spots during the job, so ask for a light check at the end of each day.

Ventilation, Air Quality, and Daily Life

Paint has a smell, especially in the first few days. Open windows when the weather allows. Run fans during and after the work. In South Florida, humidity can slow drying, so good airflow helps the paint cure well.

If anyone in your home is sensitive to odors, talk with your painter about low-odor paint options and a schedule that works for your family.

Touch-Ups and the Final Walk-Through

When the main coats are dry, the crew will look for missed spots, small drips, and tape marks. This is when touch-ups happen. Take your time on the final walk-through. Look at each room in both daylight and lamp light. Note anything that needs attention and share it with your painter.

Once the touch-ups are done, the crew will clean up and move your furniture back with you. Take a moment to check doors, windows, and cabinets. Make sure everything closes and opens smoothly.

Tips for a Smoother Project

  • Choose your colors early, and test large samples on the wall before you commit.
  • Clear personal items from shelves, closets, and counters before the crew arrives.
  • Plan for pets and kids to stay out of work zones for the full day.
  • Keep a small bag with daily essentials in a room that is not being painted.
  • Ask about the schedule each morning so you know which rooms are next.

Why the Homeowner Role Matters

The best projects have clear communication on both sides. You know how you use your home. Your painter knows how paint behaves in real rooms. When you share your goals and ask questions, the job runs more smoothly and the results feel right.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a whole-home repaint take?

The timing depends on the size of your home, the number of rooms, and the prep needed. Your painter will give you a schedule during the walkthrough so you know what to expect.

Do I need to move everything out of the house?

No. You do not need to empty the house. You do need to clear each room before work starts there. Large furniture is usually moved to the center of the room and covered.

Why do painters use so much tape and plastic?

Tape and plastic protect trim, windows, floors, and fixtures. Good masking gives you clean lines and keeps paint off surfaces that should stay untouched.

Can I stay in the house during the painting?

Yes, most homeowners stay in the home. Plan to keep work rooms clear and keep pets and kids out of active zones. Ask your painter about the daily schedule.
