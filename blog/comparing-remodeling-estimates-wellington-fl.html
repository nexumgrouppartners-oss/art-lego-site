How to Compare Remodeling Estimates in Wellington, FL

By Art Lego LLC · South Florida · November 7, 2026

Comparing remodeling estimates is easier when you know what to look for. Read each estimate line by line, check that every bid covers the same work, and ask about allowances, scope gaps and the schedule. A clear comparison helps you choose a contractor you can trust.

Start by Matching the Scope of Work

Two estimates can look far apart and still describe different projects. One bid may include demolition, haul-away and permits. Another may leave those items out. Before you compare totals, make sure each estimate describes the same rooms, the same materials and the same finish level.

Write down the scope in your own words. Include the walls, floors, cabinets, fixtures and trim you want changed. Then hold each estimate up to that list. Any item that is missing from one bid is a gap you need to ask about.

Read the Line Items Closely

A good estimate breaks the project into clear parts. You should see labor, materials, equipment, disposal and any site work listed on their own lines. Vague lines like "miscellaneous" or "general work" make it hard to know what you are paying for.

Look for these items on every estimate:

  • Demolition and removal of old materials
  • Materials, with brands or grades named when possible
  • Labor for each major phase of the work
  • Cleanup and debris removal
  • Any permit fees or inspection work
  • Protection for floors, furniture and the rest of the home

Ask How Allowances Work

Many remodeling estimates use allowances. An allowance is a set amount for items you have not yet chosen, such as tile, fixtures or cabinet hardware. If the final choice costs more than the allowance, you pay the difference. If it costs less, you may get a credit.

Ask each contractor how they set their allowances. Find out whether the amounts are realistic for the Wellington and Palm Beach County market. Ask what happens if you change your mind after work begins. A bid with low allowances can look cheaper, but the final cost may be higher.

Look for Scope Gaps

A scope gap is work that someone should do but that does not appear in the estimate. Common gaps include wall repairs after a plumbing change, electrical updates for new lights, or paint and trim work after the remodel. Gaps often show up later as change orders, and those can add stress and cost.

Ask each contractor to walk through the plan with you. Point to a wall, a ceiling or a floor and ask, "Who handles this?" A good contractor will answer clearly. If the answer is unclear, add a note to the estimate or ask for an updated version.

Also check for work that depends on other work. A new vanity may need new plumbing lines, and new cabinets may need electrical changes. Each estimate should show these links clearly, so nothing is left for later.

Compare the Schedule and the Crew Plan

The schedule matters as much as the price. Ask when work will start, how long each phase will take and what days the crew plans to work. Find out who will be on site each day and who will be your main point of contact.

Also ask how the contractor handles delays. Weather, material delivery and inspection timing can all change the plan. A contractor who explains these risks up front is usually easier to work with than one who promises a perfect timeline.

Check the Payment Terms

Payment terms should be written in the estimate or the contract. Look for a clear payment schedule tied to work milestones. Be careful with any bid that asks for most of the money before work begins. Ask what each payment covers and when it is due.

Keep copies of every estimate and every written change. A simple folder or shared file makes the comparison easier to follow as the project moves forward.

Questions to Ask Every Contractor

Use the same list of questions for each bid. This keeps the comparison fair and makes differences easy to spot.

  • What exactly is included, and what is not included?
  • How are allowances set, and what happens if I go over them?
  • Who handles permits, inspections and cleanup?
  • How long will each phase take, and who will be on site?
  • How do you handle changes after the work starts?
  • Can you share a written schedule and payment plan?

Why Local Knowledge Matters in Wellington

Homes in Wellington and across Palm Beach County face heat, humidity and salt air. These conditions affect materials, drying times and how work is sequenced. Frequently Asked Questions

Why do remodeling estimates vary so much?

Bids can describe different scopes, materials and cleanup plans. Match the scope first, then compare the totals.

What is an allowance in a remodeling estimate?

An allowance is a set amount for items you have not chosen yet, such as tile or fixtures. Ask how it works and what happens if you go over.

Should I choose the lowest estimate?

Not always. A lower bid may leave out work, use low allowances or have a weak schedule. Compare the details before you decide.
