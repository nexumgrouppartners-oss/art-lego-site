What Drives Kitchen Remodel Costs in South Florida?

By Art Lego LLC · South Florida · August 28, 2026

Kitchen remodeling pricing isn’t a one-size-fits-all number. Two kitchens that look similar at first glance can involve very different levels of work, materials, and planning. Rather than throw out a figure that won’t match your project, here’s a breakdown of what actually drives the cost of a kitchen remodel.

The Scope of Work

The biggest factor is what you’re actually doing. A cosmetic refresh—new paint, hardware, and a backsplash—is fundamentally different from reconfiguring the layout, moving plumbing, and installing new cabinetry and appliances. Know what you want to change before talking to contractors, and be clear about it. The clearer your scope, the more accurate your estimate.

Cabinets and Storage

Cabinetry is typically the largest material cost in a kitchen remodel. Stock cabinets, semi-custom, and fully custom options span a wide range, each offering different levels of finishes, durability, and design. Your choice here will move your overall project cost significantly.

Countertops and Materials

Countertop choices—laminate, butcher block, engineered quartz, natural granite, or other materials—carry different price points. Installation complexity also matters. Simpler layouts cost less than intricate designs with islands or multiple levels.

Appliances

Kitchen appliances range widely in cost depending on size, features, and brand. Energy-efficient models, smart features, and premium finishes add to the investment. Your appliance choices are often one of the easier areas to control the budget on.

Plumbing and Electrical

If you’re moving the sink, adding an island, or repositioning appliances, plumbing and electrical work increases. These are typically licensed trades that add to labor and material costs. Simple kitchens with minimal rework cost less.

Flooring

Kitchen flooring—tile, vinyl, wood, or other materials—and installation methods affect price. Some kitchens reuse existing flooring; others replace it entirely. This is another area where your choices directly affect the bottom line.

Design and Custom Features

Custom features like a built-in pantry, breakfast nook, or specialty lighting add unique touches but also add cost. Keep your design practical and aligned with your budget.

Timeline and Availability

The time your contractor can dedicate to the project, material lead times, and seasonal availability can all influence timeline and overall cost.

Getting an Accurate Estimate

The best way to understand your kitchen remodel costs is to discuss your vision with a licensed contractor. Come prepared with photos, measurements, and a clear sense of what you want to change. The more detail you provide, the more accurate the estimate will be.
