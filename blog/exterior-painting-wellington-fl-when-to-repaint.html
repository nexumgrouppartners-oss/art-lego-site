When to Repaint Your Home Exterior in Wellington, FL

By Art Lego LLC · South Florida · October 29, 2026

Most exterior paint needs attention every several years in Wellington, FL. Look for fading, chalking, peeling, and soft or cracked spots on the siding and trim. If you see them, it is time to plan a repaint before small problems turn into bigger repairs.

Why Florida homes need more frequent paint checks

Florida weather is hard on paint. Strong sun breaks down the color and the binder that holds it to the surface. Humidity keeps walls damp for long stretches. Rain and wind add more stress. Together, these forces wear paint down faster than many homeowners expect.

Wellington homes also deal with mildew. Shaded walls, north-facing sides, and areas near plants stay damp longer. Mildew can cling to the paint and make the color look dull or streaky. A regular check helps you catch these issues early.

Warning signs your exterior needs paint

Paint usually gives clear signals before it fails. Watch for these signs on each side of the house:

  • Fading. Colors look washed out, especially on the sun-facing walls. Faded paint offers less protection.
  • Chalking. A powdery residue shows up when you rub the surface with a dry hand. Chalk means the paint is breaking down.
  • Peeling or blistering. Bubbles and flaking paint expose the surface underneath. Moisture is often the cause.
  • Cracks. Hairline cracks in the paint can let water reach the siding or trim. Wider cracks need repair before painting.
  • Soft or stained wood. Trim, fascia, and window frames should feel firm. Soft spots and dark stains point to water damage.
  • Mildew. Dark spots or streaks that come back after washing can mean the paint is no longer protecting the surface.

Any one of these signs is a good reason to schedule an inspection. Several signs together usually mean the paint has reached the end of its useful life.

Check the whole house, not just one wall

Sun and moisture affect each side differently. The west and south walls often take the most sun in Florida. The north and shaded walls may hold moisture longer. Walk around the entire house and note the condition of each side.

Look at trim, eaves, soffits, and garage doors too. These areas often show wear before the main walls do. A small problem on the trim can turn into a bigger issue if it goes unchecked.

Why prep work matters most

A new coat of paint is only as good as the prep underneath. Good prep starts with a thorough cleaning. Dirt, mildew, and chalk must come off before new paint can bond. Loose or peeling paint needs to be scraped back to a firm edge. Cracks need filling, and damaged wood may need repair or replacement.

Priming also matters. Bare wood, patched areas, and surfaces that have changed color often need a primer. The primer helps the topcoat stick and look even. Ask your painter how they will prep each surface before any paint goes on.

The best season to schedule your repaint

Timing affects how well the paint cures. Paint needs dry conditions and mild temperatures to bond properly. In South Florida, the drier months often give painters the best window. Heavy rain, very high humidity, and strong storms can delay work and affect results.

Plan ahead when you can. An early schedule gives your painter room to prep properly and avoid rushed work. Ask about the forecast and how they will handle weather delays.

Choosing the right paint for Florida

Not every exterior paint performs the same in the sun. Look for products made for exterior use in hot, humid climates. Satin and semi-gloss finishes can clean up more easily than flat finishes. Lighter colors reflect more heat, which can help the paint hold up in strong sun.

Ask your painter what they recommend for your home's surfaces. Stucco, wood, fiber cement, and metal each need a different approach. The right product should match both the surface and your local weather.

What a good exterior painting project includes

A clear plan helps you compare bids and avoid surprises. Expect the painter to walk the property with you first. They should note the areas that need repair, the surfaces to prime, and the colors you have chosen. You should receive a written scope before work begins.

The scope should cover prep, repairs, primer, topcoat, and cleanup. It should also name the protection you can expect for plants, walkways, and windows during the work. Ask what happens if the weather changes midway through the job.

Questions to ask before you hire a painter

Ask how the team will prep each surface, and how many coats they plan to apply. Ask which paint brands and finishes they recommend, and why. Ask how long the work may take and how they will keep you informed. Ask who will be on site each day and who to call with questions.

It also helps to ask to see project photos from recent exterior work. Photos show the level of detail you can expect on your home.

How to budget without guessing

Exterior painting is a real investment, and the size of the job matters. The number of stories, the amount of trim, and the condition of the surfaces all shape the work. The best way to plan is to get a detailed estimate from someone who walks the property with you. A clear estimate lets you compare options side by side.

Keep in mind that the cheapest option may skip prep steps. Those steps protect your investment. Ask what is included, and make sure the scope matches what you need.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I repaint my home exterior in Wellington?

There is no single answer. Check the paint every year, and plan a repaint when you see fading, chalking, peeling, cracks, or soft trim.

What is chalking on exterior paint?

Chalking is a powdery residue that comes off when you rub the paint with a dry hand. It means the paint is breaking down under sun and weather.

Does prep matter more than the paint itself?

Prep matters a great deal. Cleaning, scraping, filling cracks, and priming help the new paint bond and last longer.

Can I schedule exterior painting during the rainy season?

It can be harder. Heavy rain and high humidity can delay work and affect curing, so ask your painter how they will handle weather changes.
