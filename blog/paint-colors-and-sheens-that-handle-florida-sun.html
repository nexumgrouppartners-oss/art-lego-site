Choosing Exterior Paint Colors and Sheens for Florida Sun

By Art Lego LLC · South Florida · November 2, 2026

Light to medium exterior colors handle Florida sun best. Satin is the most common sheen for siding, and semi-gloss works best for trim and doors. Dark colors can look great, but they absorb more heat and may show fading sooner.

Why Color Choice Matters in Florida Sun

Strong sun does more to exterior paint than most people expect. Ultraviolet light breaks down pigment over time. Heat builds up on walls that face the sun all day. Your color choice affects how hot the surface gets and how fast the color starts to look tired.

Dark colors absorb more sunlight. That extra heat can stress the paint film, especially on south and west walls. Deep reds, navy and charcoal often need more frequent touch ups than soft whites, beiges and light grays.

This does not mean every home must be white. Many mid-tone colors perform well outside. The key is to pick a shade that reflects a good amount of light and a paint line made for exterior use.

Sun Exposure on Each Side of Your Home

Not every wall gets the same sun. In Florida, the west and south walls often take the most heat. The east side gets strong morning sun, and the north side usually stays shadier. Think about each side before you choose a color.

You may pick a deeper shade for a shaded north wall. A lighter tone often works better on a hot west wall. Some homeowners use one main color on most of the house and a different accent color on the sunniest side.

Light Colors vs. Dark Colors

  • Light colors reflect more sun and usually stay cooler. They hide dust and mildew stains well. They can show dirt faster, so plan for regular cleaning.
  • Medium colors offer a good balance. Warm sand, soft gray and muted green often hold up well in the sun.
  • Dark colors give a bold look, but they absorb more heat. Use them on accents like shutters, doors and trim when you want drama without a full wall of heat.

If you love a dark shade, ask your painter about a paint with strong UV resistance. Ask about the tint base too. Some deep colors need extra coats to reach full coverage.

Flat, Satin, or Semi-Gloss: Which Sheen Fits Your Home

Sheen changes how paint looks and how it handles weather. It also changes how easy the surface is to clean. Think about both the look you want and the care you can give the paint over time.

  • Flat hides small wall flaws well, but it is harder to wash. It can hold dirt and mildew stains. Flat is less common on Florida exteriors for this reason.
  • Satin is a common pick for siding. It cleans easier than flat and still looks soft. It works well on most stucco and lap siding.
  • Semi-gloss is the toughest and easiest to wash. It is often used on doors, trim, railings and shutters. It shows surface flaws more clearly, so the prep work must be careful.

A common mix is satin on the main walls and semi-gloss on trim and doors. This gives you a clean look and tough edges where hands and weather hit the most.

Prep Matters More Than the Paint Name

Even the best exterior paint will fail on a poor surface. Old paint that is peeling, chalky or flaking needs to come off first. Mildew needs a proper cleaning and a product that slows it from coming back. Bare wood, stucco and fiber cement each need the right primer.

Florida humidity makes this step more important. Moisture can get trapped under paint. Sun and salt air also speed up failure on the south and east sides of a home. Good prep lets the paint bond and last longer.

Check HOA and Neighborhood Color Rules

Many Palm Beach County neighborhoods have homeowners associations. Some HOAs approve only certain colors or require a review before you start. Check your community rules before you buy paint. Ask for the approved color list in writing.

Even without an HOA, think about the street. A bold color can look great on its own and clash with the houses next door. A small test area on the wall helps you judge the color in morning and afternoon light.

Tips for Testing Colors on Your Home

  • Paint two large test boards, about two feet square.
  • Place one board on the sunny side and one on the shaded side.
  • Look at the colors in the morning, at midday and in the evening.
  • Wait a few days before you decide. Clouds and rain change how a color reads.
  • Write down the paint name and sheen so you can match it later.

When to Call a Local Painter

If you are unsure about color, sheen or prep, a local painter can help. A good painter will look at the sun exposure, the surface and your neighborhood rules. They can also show you finished projects in person.

Frequently Asked Questions

What exterior paint color is best for Florida sun?

Light to medium colors usually handle Florida sun best. Light shades reflect more heat. Dark shades can work too, but they absorb more heat and may fade faster.

Is satin or semi-gloss better for an exterior?

Satin is a common choice for siding because it cleans well and still looks soft. Semi-gloss is the toughest option for trim, doors and shutters.

Should I avoid flat paint outside?

Flat paint is harder to wash and can hold dirt and mildew stains. Many homeowners in Florida choose satin or semi-gloss for exterior surfaces.

Do I need HOA approval before painting?

Many communities have color rules. Check your HOA documents or ask the board for the approved color list before you buy paint.
