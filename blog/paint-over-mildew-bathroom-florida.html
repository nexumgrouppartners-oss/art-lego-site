Can You Paint Over Mildew in a Florida Bathroom?

By Art Lego LLC · South Florida · November 18, 2026

No, you should not paint straight over mildew. The mildew has to be cleaned off and the wall dried first, or it grows back through the new coat. A bathroom also needs products intended for damp rooms, because a standard flat finish can peel or mildew again.

Art Lego LLC is based in Wellington, FL, and we see this in bathrooms across Palm Beach County. Our painting services start with the wall condition, not the paint color. Below is how mildew works, what prep comes first, and what to ask before anyone opens a can.

Why does mildew keep coming back on bathroom walls?

Mildew returns because the moisture that fed it is still there. A coat of paint over a damp, dirty wall only hides the spots for a while.

Florida makes this harder. Warm, humid air meets steam from the shower, and a small room holds that moisture for hours. Mildew grows best on walls and ceilings that stay damp, especially near the shower, in corners, and above the door.

Paint that goes over living mildew gives it a protected place to grow. The dark spots show through again, often within a few months, and the finish may bubble or flake where the growth sits underneath.

What gets cleaned and dried before any paint goes on?

The surface gets cleaned of all mildew, rinsed, and left fully dry. Prep is where most bathroom paint jobs are won or lost. Here is the order we follow:

  1. Find the moisture source. Look for a leaking fixture, a dripping vent line, or a fan that does not move air.
  2. Fix the source first. Paint will not hold over a wall that keeps getting wet.
  3. Clean the mildew off. Use a cleaner made to remove mildew, and work it into the surface with a brush or sponge.
  4. Rinse well. Leftover cleaner can stop new paint from sticking.
  5. Let the wall dry completely. Run the fan and open a window if you have one. Damp drywall under a dry-looking surface still causes trouble.
  6. Scrape and sand loose paint. Peeling edges must be removed and feathered smooth.
  7. Patch and prime. Fill any holes or soft spots, then prime the bare areas with a primer suited to bathrooms.
  8. Paint in thin, even coats. Let each coat dry as the label directs.

Wear gloves and eye protection when cleaning, and keep the room ventilated. Never mix cleaning products, since some combinations give off harmful fumes.

Why can a flat finish fail in a bathroom?

A flat finish is porous and soaks up moisture, so it can peel or hold mildew in a damp room. That is why Art Lego LLC chooses products intended for damp rooms for bathroom walls and ceilings.

Flat paint looks soft and hides wall flaws, which is why many people like it in bedrooms. In a bathroom, though, steam and splashes sit on that surface. A finish with a little more sheen is easier to wipe and handles moisture better. Some paints are also made to resist mildew growth on the dried film.

For the full picture on sheen, our guide to interior painting in Florida humidity covers how finishes behave in damp homes. Ceilings matter too, since the ceiling over a shower is often the first place mildew shows.

Does the bathroom fan matter as much as the paint?

Yes, a working fan matters just as much. The best product still struggles if steam has nowhere to go after every shower.

Check that the fan pulls air. A simple test is to hold a single square of tissue near the grille and see if it holds. Run the fan during the shower and for a while after, so the walls can dry. Clean the grille if it is clogged with dust.

If the fan is weak, noisy, or not venting outside, mention it to the painter and to whoever handles your repairs. Painting a bathroom with poor airflow is a short-term fix at best.

What about mildew on the ceiling and grout lines?

Ceilings get the same cleaning and drying steps as walls, and they often need them most. Steam rises, so the ceiling stays damp longer than the lower walls.

Grout and caulk are a different matter. Mildew that has worked into old caulk usually means the caulk should be removed and replaced before painting nearby surfaces. A painter can point this out, and a bathroom remodel may be the better route if the damage goes deeper than the paint.

When is it time to call a painter instead?

Call a painter when the mildew covers a large area, keeps returning, or sits on soft or swollen drywall. Those signs point to a moisture problem that a quick scrub and coat will not solve.

It also helps to call if you are not sure what you are looking at. Discoloration can be mildew, water staining, or damage from a leak, and each one needs a different fix. Frequently Asked Questions

Can I just use mildew-blocking primer over the mold?

Primer is not a substitute for cleaning. The mildew must be removed and the wall dried first. A mildew-resistant primer can then help on the clean surface, but it will not stop growth that is still underneath.

How long should the bathroom wall dry before painting?

Wait until the surface is dry to the touch and no longer feels cool or damp. That can take longer in humid weather. Running the fan and keeping the door open helps the wall dry fully.

Is mildew on a bathroom ceiling a sign of a leak?

Not always. Steam alone can cause it. But if the stain spreads, feels soft, or appears after rain, a leak may be the cause. Fix any leak before cleaning and repainting the area.
