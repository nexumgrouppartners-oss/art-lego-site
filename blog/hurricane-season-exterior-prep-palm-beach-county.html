Preparing Your Home's Exterior for Hurricane Season in Palm Beach County

By Art Lego LLC · South Florida · September 26, 2026

Preparing your home’s exterior before hurricane season arrives in Palm Beach County means checking the details that keep wind-driven rain out: paint condition, caulking, trim, and the area immediately around your home. None of this replaces your own storm plan or official emergency guidance, but a well-maintained exterior gives your home a better chance of coming through a storm with minimal water intrusion. Here’s what to walk through before the season is in full swing.

Why Exterior Prep Matters Before Storm Season

A home’s exterior envelope—its paint, caulking, siding, and trim—is the first line of defense against wind-driven rain during a tropical system. Small gaps or failing materials that seem minor on a calm day can become a path for water once rain is being pushed sideways under pressure. Addressing these details before storm season is well underway is far more manageable than trying to fix them once a system is already approaching.

Paint and Coatings That Hold Up to Wind-Driven Rain

Exterior paint that’s already cracking, peeling, or chalking has lost some of its ability to shed water, which matters most during the exact conditions a storm brings. Refreshing paint on surfaces showing wear, and making sure any exposed wood or trim is properly primed and sealed, helps your home’s exterior perform the way it’s supposed to when heavy rain is being driven horizontally by wind.

Caulking, Sealing, and Trim Checks

Windows, doors, and any wall penetrations—vents, pipes, exterior fixtures—rely on caulking and sealant to stay watertight. Over time, South Florida’s heat and sun cause caulking to crack, shrink, or separate from the surface. Walking the exterior to check these areas and recaulking where needed is one of the most cost-effective ways to reduce the risk of water finding its way inside during a storm.

Landscaping and Debris Considerations Around the Home

Overgrown branches, loose landscaping materials, and anything that could become airborne in high winds are worth addressing before storm season. Trimming vegetation away from the roofline and siding also reduces the chance of a branch scraping paint or damaging trim during sustained winds, which is a smaller but still relevant part of overall exterior preparation.

HOA Considerations in Wellington-Area Neighborhoods

If you live in a Wellington-area community with an HOA, it’s worth checking your community’s guidelines before doing exterior prep work, since many associations have rules around approved paint colors, shutter types, and what can be stored outside during storm season. Getting ahead of these requirements avoids the frustration of redoing work or needing after-the-fact approval right when time is tight.

Post-Storm Exterior Care

Once a storm has passed, walk the exterior again for new cracks, chipped paint, loose trim, or signs that water may have gotten behind siding or around window frames. Catching and addressing minor damage early helps prevent it from turning into a larger repair before the next system moves through, which is a real possibility during an active season.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I start preparing my home's exterior for hurricane season?
It's best to walk through caulking, paint condition, and trim well before hurricane season is underway, since repairs and touch-ups take time to schedule and complete. Waiting until a storm is already approaching leaves little room for anything beyond basic securing.

Does exterior paint condition actually matter during a storm?
Yes. Paint and caulking that are already cracked, peeling, or failing give wind-driven rain more opportunities to work into siding, trim, and wall penetrations. Keeping the exterior envelope in good condition reduces one avenue for water intrusion during heavy, sideways rain.

What should I check around windows and doors before storm season?
Check the caulking and weatherstripping around every window and door for gaps, cracking, or separation, since these are common points where wind-driven rain finds its way inside. Recaulking as needed before hurricane season is a straightforward way to close off those gaps.

Do HOA rules in Wellington-area communities affect hurricane prep work?
They can, since many HOAs have guidelines around exterior paint colors, shutter types, and what can be stored or attached to the outside of a home. Checking your community's guidelines before scheduling exterior prep work helps you avoid a project that needs to be redone or approved after the fact.

What should I do to my home's exterior after a storm passes?
After a storm, walk the exterior for new cracks, chipped paint, loose trim, or areas where water may have gotten behind siding or around windows. Addressing minor damage promptly helps prevent it from turning into a bigger repair before the next storm.

A well-maintained exterior is one of the most practical things you can control before hurricane season peaks.
