Commercial Painting in Palm Beach County: What to Plan For

By Art Lego LLC · South Florida · October 30, 2026

Planning a commercial paint job takes more thought than picking a color. You need to plan the surfaces, the timing, and how the work fits around your customers and staff. This guide covers the steps that matter most for business owners in Wellington, West Palm Beach, and nearby towns.

Start With a Clear Scope

Before you ask for a quote, write down what you want painted. Make a simple list of every area. Include the lobby, offices, hallways, restrooms, and any exterior walls or signs. Note which areas customers see and which areas are back-of-house. A clear list helps the painter understand the job and gives you a fair way to compare bids.

Take photos too. Walk through the space with your phone and capture the walls, ceilings, doors, and trim. Pictures show wear and damage that is hard to describe on a phone call. If you have a floor plan, share it as well.

Check the Surfaces First

The condition of each surface decides how much prep the job needs. Paint over peeling or stained areas will not hold up for long. Good commercial painting starts with cleaning, scraping, sanding, and priming where needed. Ask the painter how they plan to handle problem spots before any paint goes on the wall.

Different surfaces need different products. Drywall, wood trim, metal doors, and masonry each take a different primer and paint. Exterior walls also face strong sun, heavy rain, and humidity, which are common in South Florida. Tell your painter what each surface is made of so they can plan the right materials.

  • Interior walls and ceilings in high-traffic hallways
  • Doors, frames, and trim that get scuffed every day
  • Restroom walls that need to handle moisture and frequent cleaning
  • Exterior walls, soffits, and sign areas

Schedule Around Business Hours

Most businesses cannot close for a full week. Many owners ask for work after hours, on weekends, or during a slower season. Night work can keep your doors open, but crews may need extra time and lighting. Talk through your hours early so the painter can build the schedule around your customers.

Plan the job in phases. Paint one wing or one floor at a time so the rest of the business keeps running. Ask how long each phase will take and when each area will be ready for use again. A written schedule keeps everyone on the same page.

Plan for Drying Time and Access

Paint needs time to dry and cure. Fresh walls can be sensitive to bumps, cleaning, and heavy furniture for a while. Ask your painter how long each coat needs before you move things back into place. Clear the area before the crew arrives so they can work quickly and safely.

Make sure the crew knows where to park, where to store supplies, and which doors they can use. Mark any areas that must stay off limits. Good access planning saves time and avoids surprises on the first day.

Pick Colors and Finishes With Care

Color choice in a business is about more than style. Light colors make small rooms feel larger and brighten a lobby. Darker colors can hide scuffs in busy halls. Sheen matters too. Flat finishes hide flaws, while satin and semi-gloss finishes are easier to wipe clean. Many owners choose a flat finish for ceilings and a satin finish for walls that get heavy use.

Bring paint samples to the space and look at them in both morning and evening light. Colors can look very different under bright Florida sun and under indoor lights. Ask for a test patch on one wall before you approve the whole job.

Walk Through the Job With Your Painter

A site walkthrough is the best way to confirm the plan. Walk each area with the painter and point out what matters most to you. Ask about prep, number of coats, and cleanup. Ask what they will do about furniture, equipment, and dust. Write down the answers and keep them with your estimate.

Talk about cleanup before the job starts. A good crew protects floors and fixtures, removes drop cloths and tape, and leaves the space ready to use. Agree on who will do a final walk-through with you before the crew leaves.

Compare Estimates the Right Way

When you compare estimates, look past the total. Check whether each one lists the same areas, the same prep steps, and the same number of coats. A low estimate that skips prep may cost more later. Ask each company what is included and what is extra. Clear written estimates make decisions easier.

Frequently Asked Questions

How far ahead should I plan a commercial paint job?

Start planning as early as you can. Walk the space, make your scope list, and talk with a painter before you pick a date. Early planning gives you more room to set a schedule that works around your customers.

Can commercial painting happen after business hours?

Yes, many businesses schedule painting at night or on weekends. Ask the painter how the after-hours plan affects the timeline and the price, and make sure the crew has access to the building.

Do I need to move furniture before the painters arrive?

Ask your painter what they will move and what you should clear. Removing small items and personal belongings helps the crew work faster and keeps your things safe from drips and dust.

What should I look for when comparing painting estimates?

Compare the areas covered, the prep work, the number of coats, and the cleanup plan. Ask what is included and what would be an extra charge. Clear written estimates make it easier to compare.
