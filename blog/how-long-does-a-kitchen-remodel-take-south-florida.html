How Long Does a Kitchen Remodel Take in South Florida?

By Art Lego LLC · South Florida · September 26, 2026

Most South Florida kitchen remodels take several weeks to a few months from the start of demolition to the final walkthrough, with the total timeline shaped by scope, material lead times, and permitting. A cosmetic update—new counters, a backsplash, and hardware—typically moves faster than a full layout change involving plumbing and electrical work. Homes in Wellington and other HOA-governed Palm Beach County communities may also need extra time built in for architectural review before work begins.

What Determines Your Kitchen Remodel Timeline

The single biggest factor in how long your kitchen remodel takes is scope. A refresh that keeps the existing layout and swaps countertops, a backsplash, and fixtures moves through the process quickly because there’s little structural work involved. A remodel that repositions the sink, moves an island, or reconfigures the footprint requires more coordination between trades—framing, plumbing, and electrical all have to happen in sequence rather than at once. The size of the kitchen, whether you’re working with stock or custom cabinetry, and how many subcontractors need to rotate through the space also affect the overall schedule.

Permitting Timelines in Palm Beach County

Depending on the scope of your project, a kitchen remodel involving electrical, plumbing, or structural changes typically requires permit review before work can begin, along with inspections at various stages of construction. Review times vary by municipality and current workload, so it’s wise to build permitting into your overall timeline rather than assume work can start the moment you sign a contract. A licensed contractor generally handles the permitting process on your behalf and can give you a realistic sense of how it fits into your specific project.

Material Lead Times and South Florida Humidity

Cabinets, countertops, and specialty fixtures often need to be ordered well before installation day, and custom or semi-custom items typically take longer to arrive than in-stock options. South Florida’s humidity is also a factor behind the scenes: wood cabinetry needs to acclimate properly and be stored correctly before installation to avoid warping or swelling once it’s in place. Experienced contractors plan delivery timing around this, so materials arrive close to when they’re actually needed rather than sitting in humid conditions for weeks.

HOA Review in Wellington-Area Communities

Many neighborhoods in and around Wellington are governed by homeowners associations with architectural review committees, and while most kitchen remodels are entirely interior, any change visible from outside—like new venting or an exterior wall modification—may need HOA approval before demolition starts. Submitting your plans for review as early as possible, ideally before you finalize a start date with your contractor, helps prevent a project from stalling out while waiting on committee sign-off.

Hurricane Season Scheduling Considerations

Kitchen remodels that fall during hurricane season need a bit more flexibility built into the schedule. Material deliveries can be delayed ahead of a storm, and crews may need to pause work if a watch or warning is issued for the area. Planning for a temporary kitchen setup and avoiding a start date that lands right before a forecasted storm are both reasonable ways to keep a project from losing significant time to weather.

A Typical Week-by-Week Timeline

While every project is different, a typical—not guaranteed—kitchen remodel timeline often looks something like this: planning and material selection happen before any demolition begins, followed by a couple of weeks for demolition and any plumbing or electrical rough-in. Cabinet and countertop installation generally follows once materials arrive, and the final stretch covers painting, backsplash, fixtures, and appliance hookup. Smaller refreshes can move through this sequence in a shorter span, while full remodels with structural changes take longer at each stage.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a full kitchen remodel take in South Florida?
A full kitchen remodel that touches layout, plumbing, and electrical typically runs several weeks to a few months, while a cosmetic refresh with new counters, backsplash, and hardware usually wraps up much faster. Scope, material lead times, and permitting all shape the actual schedule.

Does a kitchen remodel take longer in a Wellington HOA community?
It can, if the project involves anything visible from outside the home, such as venting or exterior wall changes, since many Wellington-area HOAs require architectural review before work begins. Submitting your plans to the HOA early helps avoid delays once your contractor is ready to start.

How does hurricane season affect kitchen remodel scheduling?
Hurricane season can affect material deliveries and crew availability, and an active storm watch or warning may pause work for safety. Building a buffer into your schedule and avoiding a start date right before a forecasted storm helps keep the project moving.

What's the fastest way to keep my kitchen remodel timeline on track?
Finalize your material selections and design decisions before demolition starts, order custom items like cabinets as early as possible, and keep communication open with your contractor about permit status and delivery dates. Clear decisions up front prevent the delays that come from changing your mind mid-project.

Do custom cabinets add time to my kitchen remodel?
Yes, custom and semi-custom cabinetry generally takes longer to arrive than in-stock options, so ordering them as soon as your design is finalized helps keep the rest of the project on schedule. Your contractor can sequence other work while cabinets are in production.

Every kitchen remodel timeline is different, and the best way to get a realistic schedule for your home is to talk through your project with a licensed contractor.
