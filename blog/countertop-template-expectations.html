Countertop Template Day: What to Expect in Palm Beach Gardens

By Art Lego LLC · South Florida · November 17, 2026

Countertop template day is the visit where exact measurements of your installed cabinets are recorded so the new tops can be cut to fit. Cabinets and layout must be final before it happens. Once the template is made, changes get hard. This guide covers what to prepare, what to expect and what to settle early.

Art Lego LLC handles kitchen work as part of our remodeling services, and the template visit is one of the steps where a little planning saves a lot of trouble. Here is how it works for homeowners in Palm Beach Gardens and nearby.

What is a countertop template?

A template is a precise record of the shape of your cabinet tops, walls and cutouts. It is the pattern the countertop is cut from. Think of it as a custom map of your kitchen, made after the cabinets are in.

Some shops make a physical template from thin strips or board. Others measure with a digital tool. Either way, the goal is the same: the stone or surface should match your kitchen as it really is, not as the drawings said it would be. Walls are rarely perfectly straight, and cabinets can sit slightly differently than planned. The template captures that.

What has to be in place before template day?

The cabinets must be installed, level and fastened in their final position. If a cabinet shifts after the template is made, the countertop will not fit the way it should.

Use this checklist before the visit:

  • All base cabinets are set, leveled and secured to the wall.
  • Doors and drawers are installed or at least adjusted, so overhang can be checked.
  • The layout is final, including any island and where it sits.
  • The sink and cooktop models are chosen, and the spec sheets are on hand.
  • Faucet and accessory holes are decided.
  • Appliances that sit between or beside cabinets are on site or their sizes are confirmed.
  • The kitchen is clear enough to reach every run of cabinets.

Our approach to kitchens is cabinetry and surfaces planned around how the family cooks and entertains. That planning is what makes template day simple. When the layout was settled early, there is nothing left to guess at.

What does the template visit involve?

The visit is mostly measuring, marking and asking questions. Someone checks every cabinet run, corner, wall and cutout location. Expect them to look at overhang, edges and any place the top meets a wall or appliance.

Here is the usual order of the visit:

  1. They walk the kitchen and confirm the plan with you.
  2. They check that the cabinets are level and secure.
  3. They record each run, corner and wall line.
  4. They mark the sink, cooktop and faucet positions using your actual models.
  5. They confirm edge style, overhang and seam locations.
  6. They note anything that could cause a problem at install.

Be home, or send someone who can make decisions. Small questions come up, such as where a seam should fall or how far a top should overhang an island. Answering on the spot keeps things moving.

How do sink and cooktop cutouts get decided?

Cutouts are based on the actual sink and cooktop you bought, not a guess. Different sinks need different cutout sizes, and an undermount sink is handled differently from a drop-in. Bring the spec sheet or the box.

If you have not picked the sink yet, wait on the template. Changing a sink after the cutout is made can mean a new countertop. The same goes for the faucet, since the number of holes depends on the style. Soap dispensers and filtered water taps add holes too.

What happens between template and install?

After the template, the shop fabricates your countertop from it. Your job during that time is to leave the cabinets alone. Do not move them, shim them or add trim that changes the edge.

This is also the stretch to prepare for install day. Plan to keep the path from the door to the kitchen clear, because slabs are heavy and need a clean route. Ask the fabricator how they handle the old tops, the plumbing disconnect and the final sealing, so you know who is responsible for each part. We will not promise a set number of days here, because timing depends on the material and the shop.

What should you decide ahead of time?

Decide the sink, faucet, cooktop, edge profile and backsplash plan before template day. These choices change what gets measured and cut.

Here are the decisions worth settling early:

  • Sink and faucet. Pick the exact models so the cutout and holes match.
  • Cooktop or range. A slide-in range and a drop-in cooktop need different tops.
  • Edge profile. A simple edge and a decorative edge are cut differently.
  • Backsplash. Decide if it is tile, a stone strip or paint, since it changes the back edge.
  • Overhang at the island. Stools need legroom, so think about seating.
  • Seams. Ask where they will fall and why.

If you are still comparing materials, our post on kitchen countertop materials can help before you commit. Changing the material after a template can mean starting over.

What should you ask the fabricator?

Ask who handles the measuring, how seams are planned, and what happens if a measurement is off. Clear answers up front prevent surprises.

Good questions include whether the template is physical or digital, who is responsible if the cabinets move, and what the fabricator needs from you on the day. Ask who is on site for install, and whether the sink and faucet are set by them or by your plumber. If something is unclear, ask again until it is not.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to be home for the countertop template?

It helps a lot. Small decisions come up, like seam placement or island overhang. If you cannot be there, send someone you trust to decide for you, and share your choices in writing beforehand.

Can I change my sink after the template is made?

It is risky. The sink cutout is based on the model you chose, so a different sink may need a new cutout or even a new countertop. Pick the sink and faucet before template day.

Should the backsplash be installed before the template?

Usually not. The countertop goes in first and the backsplash follows. Decide the backsplash plan before the template so the back edge of the top is cut to suit it.

What if my walls are not straight?

That is normal, and it is why a template exists. The measurements capture the real wall lines so the countertop fits closely. Tell the person measuring about any known problem spots.
