Shower Waterproofing Questions for Wellington Homeowners

By Art Lego LLC · South Florida · November 16, 2026

A shower needs waterproofing behind the tile, because humidity and water will find any weak spot in a poor base. Tile and grout shed most water, not all of it. The membrane and the base underneath do the real protecting. Ask about both before any remodeling work starts.

Art Lego LLC is based in Wellington, FL, and we hear the same handful of shower questions from homeowners across Palm Beach County. Here are the ones that come up most, with plain answers.

Why does a Wellington bathroom need waterproofing?

Florida homes face wet weather for much of the year, and bathrooms add daily steam on top of it.

Because a Wellington bathroom deals with summer storms and humidity that is hard on paint and bathrooms. Moist air hangs around long after the shower is off. Any gap in the wet area gives that moisture a path into the wall.

Our Wellington page notes the same climate points. A shower built for a dry climate is not enough here. The wet area should be planned as a system, not just a tile choice.

What sits behind the tile?

Many homeowners only see the finished surface, so the hidden layers are the part worth asking about.

Behind the tile sits a solid backing, then a waterproofing layer, then the tile bed or adhesive. Each layer has a job. The backing gives a flat, stable surface. The waterproofing layer keeps water from reaching the framing. The tile and grout finish the look and shed most of the spray.

When you ask about the membrane, ask what product is used, where it runs, and how the corners and the drain area are treated. Those spots are where leaks usually start. A good crew can describe them without guessing.

What happens if the base is poor?

A poor base lets water through slowly, and the damage stays hidden for a long time. You may see loose tile, stained grout, soft drywall, or a musty smell outside the shower. By then the wall behind it may already be wet.

Uneven backing, missed corners and a rushed drain connection are common causes. Fixing them later means opening the wall again. Doing the base carefully the first time costs far less effort than tearing out finished tile.

Slope matters too. The shower floor should lead water toward the drain instead of letting it sit in corners. Standing water over time stresses grout and the layers beneath it.

Does ventilation matter?

Yes. Waterproofing handles water that reaches the wall, while a working exhaust fan handles the humid air in the room. Without it, moisture settles on paint, ceilings and trim and can lead to peeling and mildew.

During a bathroom remodel we look at the fan along with the shower. Ask whether the fan vents outside the house, whether it suits the room size, and whether the ceiling paint is a moisture-ready product. Our painting service can handle that finishing step.

What should the walk-through cover?

The walk-through should cover the scope in writing: what comes out, what the base will be, how the shower is waterproofed, and how the drain, niche and corners are handled. A clear scope means no surprises once the tile is up.

  • The backing material and how it is attached
  • The waterproofing product and where it runs
  • Treatment of corners, seams and the drain
  • Any shower niche or bench details
  • Ventilation and ceiling finish

Take photos of the wall once it is opened and again after the waterproofing step, before tile covers it. Those pictures give you a record of what sits behind the finished surface. A crew that is happy to show you the work in progress is usually a crew that took care with it.

If an item is missing from the scope, ask for it to be added before work begins. That one step prevents most disagreements later.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I just add sealer to my old shower walls?

Sealer helps grout and some tile surfaces, but it does not replace a waterproofing layer behind the tile. If the wall behind is already wet or soft, sealer will not fix it. Have the shower looked at first.

How do I know if my shower is leaking behind the wall?

Signs include loose or cracked tile, grout that keeps staining, soft or bubbling drywall near the shower, and a musty smell. If you see any of these, have it checked before the damage spreads.

Should I ask who does the waterproofing work?

Yes. Ask who installs the base and the waterproofing layer, and whether the same crew sets the tile. A clear answer shows the work is planned, not passed along without oversight.

Does a bigger shower need a different approach?

The same layers apply, but a larger or custom shower has more seams, corners and drain details to treat. Ask how those areas are handled in your specific plan.
