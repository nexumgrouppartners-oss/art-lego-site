Do You Need a Permit for a Home Remodel in Palm Beach County?

By Art Lego LLC · South Florida · November 3, 2026

Do you need a permit for a home remodel in Palm Beach County? Many remodeling projects do need one. The answer depends on the type of work, the size of the job, and the city or county where your home sits.

Why permits matter for your remodel

A building permit is official approval for work on your home. It tells the local building department what you plan to change. It also lets an inspector check the work at key stages, so the job is done safely.

Skipping a permit can cause real problems. The city or county may ask you to open up finished walls so an inspector can look. An unpermitted project can also complicate a home sale, insurance questions, or a future renovation. Those headaches are easy to avoid if you plan ahead.

Jobs that often need a permit

Every city and county sets its own list. Still, some types of work commonly need a permit in Wellington, West Palm Beach, Boynton Beach, and across Palm Beach County:

  • Moving, adding, or removing walls
  • Adding a room, a bathroom, or any new square footage
  • New or changed electrical wiring, outlets, or panels
  • New or moved plumbing lines, such as a new sink or shower location
  • Changes to windows, exterior doors, or roof structure
  • Any work that changes the size or shape of the home

Jobs that often do not need one

Some smaller projects usually do not need a permit. Examples include painting a room, replacing cabinet doors or hardware, and swapping light fixtures on an existing circuit. Even so, ask first. A quick call costs little, and rules can change from one place to the next.

How to check before you start

Use this simple process before any work begins:

  • Write down exactly what you plan to change, room by room.
  • Find the building department for your city. If you live outside a city limit, the county usually handles permits.
  • Ask whether your project needs a permit, and what drawings or plans they want to see.
  • Ask which stages need an inspection, so you can plan the schedule.
  • Check with your homeowners association, if you have one. It may have its own approval steps.

Keep notes from each call. Write down the name of the person you spoke with and the date. That record helps if questions come up later.

Who pulls the permit?

In many projects, the contractor pulls the permit. In others, the homeowner does. Ask this question before you sign anything. Make sure it is clear who applies, who pays the permit fee, and who schedules the inspections. A clear answer now prevents delays later.

Plan your timeline around permits

Permits take time. Build the review and approval steps into your schedule, especially for a kitchen or bathroom remodel. Plan the work so inspections happen at the right points, such as before walls are closed up. Waiting until the drywall is in place to call for an inspection can cost you time.

If you are still deciding on the scope of your project, a clear plan helps everyone. Our remodeling services cover general, kitchen, bathroom, interior, and exterior work. Share your plan with your contractor early, so they can point out any permit needs before you commit.

Common permit mistakes to avoid

Homeowners tend to make the same few mistakes. The first is assuming a small job is exempt without asking. Even a simple change can touch wiring or plumbing that needs review. The second is starting work before the permit is approved. Work that begins too early can be stopped, reviewed, and delayed.

The third mistake is not planning for inspections. An inspector needs a clear view of the work, so leave access to panels, pipes, and framing open until the visit is done. The last mistake is relying on a verbal answer. Phone advice is helpful, but ask for written confirmation of what your project requires.

What to bring to the permit office

Being prepared speeds up the process. Bring a clear description of the work and a rough sketch that shows the rooms and walls involved. Include the property address and parcel number, if you have them. List the materials and fixtures you plan to use, such as the type of water heater or electrical panel. The more complete your packet, the fewer questions the reviewer will need to ask.

Keep copies of everything you submit. Save the approved permit and inspection records in a folder with your home papers. These documents can be useful when you sell the home, refinance, or plan your next project.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a permit to paint my home?

Interior painting usually does not need a permit. Exterior painting can have different rules, and so can painting that goes along with other structural work. Check with your local building department and your homeowners association, if you have one.

Who usually pulls the permit for a remodel?

It depends on the project and your contract. Many contractors pull permits for the work they do, but some homeowners do it themselves. Ask before you sign, and get the answer in writing.

What happens if I remodel without a permit?

The city or county may ask you to open the work for inspection, correct it, or pay penalties. Unpermitted work can also raise questions when you sell the home.

How do I find out which permits I need?

Call or visit your city or county building department. Describe the project in detail and ask which permits and inspections it requires.
