What to Expect During a Bathroom Remodel in Palm Beach County

By Art Lego LLC · South Florida · November 5, 2026

A bathroom remodel in Palm Beach County usually moves through the same steps, in the same order. The work starts with demolition, moves through plumbing and electrical rough-in, then waterproofing, tile, fixtures, and a final punch list. Knowing this order helps you plan your schedule and your daily life at home.

Every bathroom is different, so your project may vary. Still, the basic path is the same in Wellington, West Palm Beach, Boynton Beach and the other nearby cities. Here is what to expect at each stage.

Step 1: Planning and Layout

Before any tools come out, decide what you want to change. Will the tub stay, or will it become a walk-in shower? Do you need a double vanity or more storage? Will the door swing stay where it is? These choices shape every step that follows. Changing the layout later costs more than settling it on paper first.

Plan the materials too. Tile, vanities, shower glass and faucets can take time to arrive. Ordering them early keeps the job from stopping in the middle.

Step 2: Demolition

Demolition is the loud part of the job. The crew removes the old tub or shower, vanity, toilet, flooring and wall coverings. This is also when hidden problems show up. Old subfloor, soft wood or outdated wiring can appear once the walls are open. A good crew stops and talks with you before making any change you did not expect.

Step 3: Plumbing and Electrical Rough-In

Rough-in means running the pipes, drains, vents and wires before the walls close up. The plumber checks drain slope and the location of the shower valve, toilet and vanity supply lines. The electrician places outlets, switches, lights and the exhaust fan. Your local building department may need to inspect this work before the walls are covered.

Step 4: Waterproofing

Waterproofing is one of the most important steps in a shower. Water reaches the wall every day, and a proper system keeps that moisture out of the framing. Check that the shower pan, wall membrane and corners are sealed before any tile goes up. Ask your contractor to show you the waterproofing before it is covered. Photos are a good record to keep for later.

Step 5: Backer Board and Tile

Once the waterproofing is in place, the walls get cement backer board. Then the tile goes up. Tile work depends on careful layout. The installer finds the center line and checks for level as the work goes along. Some crews set the floor first and others start on the walls, depending on the design. Grout and sealer come last, after the tile has set. Save a few extra tiles for future repairs.

Step 6: Fixtures and Finish Work

After the tile and grout are done, the fixtures go in. This includes the vanity, toilet, shower trim, faucets, showerhead, mirror and light fixtures. Paint is usually finished before the vanity goes in, so the walls are easy to reach. Doors, trim and hardware come near the end. This is the stage where the bathroom starts to look finished.

Step 7: The Punch List

A punch list is a final walk-through. You and the crew look at every detail together. Check that the faucets run, the drains flow, the fan works, the caulk lines are clean and the grout has no gaps. Mark anything that needs attention. Then review the full list with your contractor so every item gets handled.

Keeping the Project on Track

Most delays come from waiting, not from the work itself. A tile order that arrives late can hold up the whole crew. A decision about a vanity or a light fixture can stop the job for days. Make a shared list of open decisions, and give each one an owner and a due date.

Ask your contractor how changes are handled. A change to the layout after the walls are closed is harder and costs more than a change made during planning. Agree on a process for approving changes before the work starts. This keeps surprises small and the project moving.

Plan for the unexpected. Older homes in Palm Beach County often have surprises behind the walls. Keep some room in your plan for repairs that appear only after demolition. Talk through these possibilities with your crew before the first wall comes down.

Tips for Living Through the Work

  • Plan for one bathroom being out of use. Set up a simple space in another part of the house.
  • Ask how dust will be controlled. Plastic sheeting and a clear work path help a lot.
  • Keep pets and children away from open work areas and tools.
  • Ask for a written list of decisions you need to make, and the date each one is needed.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the first step in a bathroom remodel?

The first step is planning. Decide on the layout, the fixtures and the materials before demolition starts. Clear decisions early help the work move without long pauses.

Why is waterproofing so important in a shower?

Water reaches the walls every day. Waterproofing keeps moisture out of the wall framing. Check the pan, membrane and corners before tile goes up.

What is a punch list?

A punch list is a final walk-through of the finished bathroom. You and the crew check each detail, such as faucets, drains, the fan, caulk and grout, and fix any items found.
