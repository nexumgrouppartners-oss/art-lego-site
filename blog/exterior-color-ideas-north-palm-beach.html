Exterior Repaint Color Ideas for Florida Homes

By Art Lego LLC · South Florida · November 15, 2026

Choose an exterior palette that suits your neighborhood and the Florida sun, test it on your own wall, and check association rules before you buy paint. Color looks different in strong light, so a small chip is never enough. Art Lego LLC helps homeowners work through this step by step, starting with the exterior painting process.

How do you choose an exterior palette for Florida sun?

Start with the roof, the stone and the pavers, because those do not change. Pick a main body color that works with them, then add a trim color and a door color.

Strong sun changes how paint reads. A color that looks soft on a small chip can look much brighter once it covers a whole wall. Very dark colors can also look different at noon than they do in the evening.

Light reflectance is the idea to keep in mind. It describes how much light a color bounces back. Lighter colors reflect more sunlight and often look cleaner on a sunny wall. Deeper colors absorb more light and can show chalking or fading sooner on walls that face the afternoon sun.

Here is a simple way to think about it:

  • Light and warm tones tend to look clean and calm in bright sun.
  • Mid tones hide dust and salt haze better than pure white.
  • Deep tones look rich but ask more from the paint on sun-facing walls.

What does the association need to see?

Many neighborhoods in Palm Beach County have an association or architectural review board, and some ask you to submit your colors before painting. Check your rules first, because a rejected color after the work is done is hard to fix.

Ask the association what they want to receive. Most ask for the exact color names and codes, and some want a sample or a photo of the house. Ask how long the review takes, since approval timing can affect when a paint job can start.

Which finishes hold up outside?

For most exterior walls, a flat or low sheen hides surface flaws, while a satin or semi-gloss on trim wipes clean more easily. The right finish depends on the surface and how much weather it takes.

Sheen changes color. A glossier finish reflects more light and can make a color look lighter. A flat finish looks deeper and softer. When you test a color, test it in the finish you plan to use, not just on a paper chip.

Here is how the common choices compare:

  • Flat or matte: hides small wall flaws, but can hold dirt more than a smoother finish.
  • Low sheen: a common middle choice for stucco and siding, with easier cleaning.
  • Satin or semi-gloss: often used on trim, doors and fascia where wiping clean matters.

Florida humidity and sun are hard on all of them. Good prep matters as much as the color, so ask how cracks, peeling and chalky surfaces will be handled before paint goes on.

Where does the color consultation fit?

The color consultation comes early, before you commit to a palette and before you submit anything to your association. Art Lego LLC offers Free estimates and color consultation for painting jobs, so you can talk through options while the estimate is happening.

This is a good time to bring photos of homes you like, your roof and paver samples, and any rules from your association. Talk about which walls get the most sun, which areas stay shaded, and how much contrast you want between body and trim.

The goal is to leave with two or three real options, not a hundred. A short list is easier to test and easier to submit for approval.

How do you test a color?

Paint a large sample on the actual wall and watch it through a full day. Do not trust a small chip held up to the siding, because it cannot show how the color behaves in real light.

Follow these steps:

  1. Paint a sample at least a couple of feet wide on the wall, not on loose board only.
  2. Put a second sample on a shaded side of the house.
  3. Hold your trim color next to the body color to see the contrast.
  4. Look at the samples in morning light, midday sun and late afternoon.
  5. Look again on a cloudy day and after dark with the exterior lights on.
  6. Check the colors beside the roof, the pavers and the front door.

If a color feels too bright at noon but looks right in the evening, try one step softer. Colors nearly always look stronger once they cover a full house.

Trim and door contrast

Trim should be clearly different from the body, without a harsh jump. A white or soft cream trim is common on Florida homes, while a deeper trim can work on a lighter wall. The front door is the place to add a stronger color if you want one.

Look at the whole street

Walk down your street and see what the neighbors have. You do not need to match them, but you want your colors to sit comfortably next to the homes beside you. Many associations care about this too.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I choose a lighter or darker exterior color in Florida?

Lighter colors reflect more sun and often look cleaner on bright walls. Darker colors can look rich, but they absorb more light and may show fading sooner on walls that face strong afternoon sun.

Do I need approval from my association before painting?

Many neighborhoods ask for color approval first. Check your rules and ask what they need, such as color names, codes or a sample, and how long the review usually takes.

How big should a paint sample be?

Make it large, at least a couple of feet wide, and paint it on the actual wall. A small chip cannot show how the color looks across a whole house in changing light.
