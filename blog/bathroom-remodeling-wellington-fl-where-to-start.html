Bathroom Remodeling in Wellington, FL: Where to Start

By Art Lego LLC · South Florida · October 29, 2026

Bathroom remodeling goes best when you make the big decisions first. Start with how you use the room, what you want to keep, and what needs to change. Then you can plan tile, fixtures, and layout with fewer surprises.

Start with how you use the bathroom

Think about who uses the room every morning. A busy household needs a different bathroom than a couple who wants a quiet, calm space. Write a short list of daily needs. Do you need a second sink? More counter space? A seat in the shower? An entry that is easy to step through?

This list will guide almost every choice that follows. It also helps you tell a contractor what matters most, so the plan fits your home and not just the photos you saved. Our bathroom remodeling services start with this kind of conversation, so you know what each choice means before anything is built.

Keep your budget in mind as you make these choices. Each decision, from the shower layout to the vanity size, changes how much material and labor the job needs. Set priorities early so you know which features matter most and where you can be flexible.

Decide early if plumbing will move

Moving a toilet, a tub drain, or a shower drain takes more work than replacing a fixture in the same spot. Walls may need to be opened. Floors may need new framing or venting. Each move adds time and planning.

Ask yourself if the layout can stay where it is. If you want to move plumbing, bring it up at the start of the project, not halfway through. Older homes in Wellington and across Palm Beach County can hide surprises behind the walls, so keep some room in your schedule for those. Choose a shower or tub layout

A tub makes sense if you like to soak or if you bathe young children. A shower takes up less floor space and is often easier to step into. In a small bathroom, a shower with a clear glass panel can make the room feel larger and brighter.

Measure before you decide. Check the door swing, the shower size, and where the controls will sit. If you can, stand in the space with a tape measure and picture your normal morning routine.

Pick tile and flooring with care

Tile is one of the first things people notice. Choose a floor tile with a texture that gives good grip when it is wet. Shower walls need materials that can handle daily water, and Florida humidity is hard on finishes that are not made for wet rooms.

Look at tile samples in your bathroom light, not only in a showroom. Order a few extra pieces for future repairs. Tile lines that match in color and size will look better once the room is finished.

Grout deserves attention too. Light grout shows dirt faster, while darker grout hides it but can be harder to match later. Natural stone needs more care than porcelain or ceramic, so ask about sealing before you choose it for a wet area.

Plan the vanity and storage

The vanity is the center of most bathrooms. Measure the wall space before you pick a size, and leave enough room to open drawers and doors fully. Deep drawers often hold more than open shelves.

Tall cabinets and recessed medicine cabinets add storage without taking up floor space. Set the counter height so the sink is comfortable for everyone who uses it. A quick check with the people in your home can save a redo later.

Think about towel bars and hooks too. Place them where you will reach for them, such as beside the shower and the sink. Hooks on the back of the door work well in small rooms and keep the walls clear.

Think about light and ventilation

Good light makes a small bathroom feel bigger. It also helps with shaving, makeup, and daily tasks. Plan for light at the mirror, not just one ceiling fixture.

Many homeowners also like a dimmer switch and a night light. These small touches make early morning and late night trips easier and safer.

Ventilation matters too. A strong exhaust fan removes steam and helps protect paint and drywall from moisture. Make sure the fan vents outside, not into the attic.

Put the steps in order

Once the layout and finishes are chosen, put the work in order. Demolition comes first. Then come plumbing and electrical work, then walls and floors, and finally fixtures and paint. Ask how each step affects the next one. Clear steps make it easier to know what to expect each week.

Keep a list of questions. How will you use the bathroom while the work is in progress? Will you need another bathroom nearby? What happens if the team finds damage behind the walls? Good answers help you feel confident before work begins.

What to gather before you call

Before you reach out, take a few photos of your current bathroom. Write down what you like and what bothers you. Note any leaks, soft spots in the floor, or mold you have seen. Bring your ideas for tile, color, and storage.

This information helps the estimate match your goals from the start. It also gives you a clear picture of what the project will include.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I choose tile before I pick a layout?

No. Choose the layout first. The shower size, tub location, and vanity placement shape how much tile you need and where it goes.

Is it better to keep a tub or switch to a shower?

It depends on how you use the room. A tub works well for soaking and bath time with children. A shower often saves floor space and is easier to step into.

Does moving a toilet or drain add work to a remodel?

Yes. Moving plumbing usually means opening walls or floors and adding more planning. It is best to decide before the project starts.
