Questions to Ask Before Hiring a Painter in Wellington, FL

By Art Lego LLC · South Florida · November 4, 2026

Before you hire a painter in Wellington, ask about prep work, paint, coats, cleanup, schedule, and a written estimate. A good painter answers each question clearly and without pressure. If the answers are vague, keep looking. This guide explains what to ask and why each question matters for your home.

Why the Questions Matter

Painting looks simple from the outside. Most of the work happens before the first stroke. Surfaces need to be cleaned, repaired, sanded, and primed. The paint matters too. When you ask the right questions early, you know exactly what the job includes. You also avoid surprises halfway through.

1. What Prep Work Is Included?

Prep is the part that affects how the paint looks years from now. Ask the painter to walk you through each step. Good prep often includes:

  • Moving furniture and covering floors and fixtures
  • Patching holes and cracks
  • Sanding rough spots and glossy trim
  • Washing walls, especially in kitchens and bathrooms
  • Priming bare wood, new drywall, and stained areas

If prep is not listed in the estimate, ask why. A quote that leaves out prep may look simple, but the finish may not hold up.

2. Which Paint Will Be Used?

Ask which paint line and sheen the painter plans to use for each surface. Then ask why. A good answer explains the choice. A hallway that gets constant use needs a different finish than a ceiling. Exterior walls in Palm Beach County face strong sun, heavy rain, and salt air. Those walls need paint made for those conditions.

Ask for the product name in writing. That lets you check it yourself. Also confirm that the paint in the estimate is the same paint that arrives on the job site.

3. How Many Coats Are Included?

Many jobs need two coats of finish paint. Dark colors over light colors, or bare drywall, may need more. Ask how many coats the estimate includes. Ask whether primer counts as one of them. Get the answer in writing so you can compare estimates side by side.

4. What Does Cleanup Look Like?

Painting leaves dust, drips, and tape residue. Ask who removes the painter's tape, drop cloths, and trash. Ask whether paint is cleaned from floors, windows, and fixtures. Ask what happens to furniture that was moved. A clear cleanup plan saves you time after the crew leaves.

5. What Is the Schedule?

Ask for a start date and a rough end date. Ask how many days the crew expects to work. Ask what happens if weather delays an exterior job. In South Florida, afternoon storms can slow outdoor work. A good painter plans around the weather and tells you about changes early.

6. Is the Estimate Written and Detailed?

A written estimate protects you and the painter. It should list the rooms or walls, the surfaces, the prep steps, the paint, the number of coats, and the cleanup. It should also show the start date and payment terms. If a bid is only one number on a sticky note, ask for a full breakdown.

7. Who Will Be on the Job?

Ask who will lead the crew and who you should call with questions. Ask whether the painters are employees or subcontractors. Ask how the crew protects your home and keeps you informed. One clear point of contact makes the job smoother for everyone.

8. Can You See Past Work?

Ask to see project photos of work similar to yours. Look closely at trim lines, corners, and ceilings. Photos on a company's Google Business Profile are a good place to start. Compare what you see with the job you want done.

How to Prepare for the Estimate Visit

Walk the space with the painter before they quote. Point out each room, wall, and trim line you want painted. Note any cracks, stains, peeling spots, or areas that bother you. Show the colors you are considering, and bring paint chips or a sample board if you have them. This helps the painter give you an accurate number and reduces the chance of extra charges later.

Also share your timeline. If you have guests, a move-in date, or a holiday gathering, say so. A painter who knows your deadline can plan the prep, the drying time, and the crew size to fit it. Ask them to note any restrictions in the written estimate, such as work hours or pet care.

Red Flags to Watch For

  • No written estimate
  • Pressure to sign right away
  • Vague answers about prep or paint
  • Requests for cash-only payment
  • No clear start date or cleanup plan

One of these signs may not mean a bad painter. A pattern of them should make you pause.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should a painting estimate include?

A good estimate lists the rooms or surfaces, the prep steps, the paint, the number of coats, the cleanup, and a start date. It should be in writing.

Should I ask about primer?

Yes. Ask whether primer is included and whether it counts as one of the coats. Primer matters most on bare drywall, raw wood, and stained areas.

How long does a painting job take?

It depends on the size of the home, the amount of prep, and the weather. Ask the painter for a schedule with a start date and a rough end date.

Do I need to be home during painting?

Not always. Plan for moved furniture, paint smell, and access to rooms. Ask the painter what they need from you before the crew arrives.
